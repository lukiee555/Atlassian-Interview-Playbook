Master project A: Pricing continuity and derived-price correctness

Default deep diveBackend + distributed systems

Safe fallback modes in a multi-stage pricing pipeline

Business narrative

A legacy multi-stage flow needed safe replay/rollback and a reliable derived national price based on current regional values.

Technical narrative

Stage-aware routing, explicit operating modes, tenant-scoped reads, no-ratchet aggregation, per-offer isolation, idempotency and retriable failure.

People narrative

You owned design through deployment, documented trade-offs, sought review and aligned business correctness with operational continuity.

Result

Simplified legacy handling, improved derived-price accuracy and created safer recovery controls with broad automated coverage.

Decision tree to explain

  • Why explicit modes? Normal traffic, controlled replay and emergency bypass have different risk profiles.
  • Why stage-aware routing? The same consumer handled multiple stages; already-processed stage-two traffic must never enter the substitute path.
  • Why database over local cache? Multiple instances across clusters made local state incoherent; the customer-visible value required current data.
  • Why exclude the previous derived value? Feeding the old derived result back into the maximum would create a ratchet that could rise but not correctly fall.
  • Why partial-batch isolation? One failing offer should not silently drop every later independent offer.
  • Why retry rather than degrade? Batch-only candidates might look valid while omitting a higher persisted price.
Twenty follow-up directions

Prepare: business rule, stages, event flow, concurrency, database query, index, tenancy, cache rejection, failure semantics, idempotency, replay behavior, DLQ, duplicate publication trade-off, test strategy, feature flags, rollback, observability, open risk, what you would change and production evidence.

Confidentiality

Use generalized identifiers in a public interview. Keep exact store numbers, internal config keys, repository/branch names and private topology out of the answer unless your employer permits disclosure.

Master project B: Spark consolidation

Initiative165M+ records

Five workflows to one configurable pipeline

Business

Duplicated workflows increased change cost and operational load.

Technical

Behavior mapping, configurable variants, reconciliation, staged rollout and scale validation.

People

Made the migration safe enough for stakeholders to adopt rather than demanding simplification on principle.

Result

One pipeline processed 165M+ records and replaced five jobs.

Follow-ups

  • Which differences were genuine business variants?
  • How did you compare old and new outputs?
  • What was the rollback plan?
  • Did one job create a larger blast radius?
  • What operational or cost metric improved?
  • What old code and schedules were retired?

Master project C: Pricing migration and international integration

Cross-team judgmentMigration + tenancy

Observable migration across services and markets

Business

Move a customer-critical read/data path without allowing invisible pricing differences.

Technical

Control, parallel-comparison and target modes; tenant-aware contracts; feature controls and rollback.

People

Aligned dependent teams on headers, events, validation, failure behavior and rollout order.

Result

Created evidence before cutover and reduced integration ambiguity.

Follow-ups

  • What was compared and what mismatch threshold was acceptable?
  • How did you avoid temporary modes becoming permanent?
  • Which teams or services owned each handoff?
  • What happened when tenant context was absent or inconsistent?
  • How did you roll back?

Master project D: Promo operations UI under urgency

Product ownershipTwo-day lifecycle

From script handoff to usable production workflow

Business

A process gap had to be corrected before Monday so promotional pricing could be updated.

Technical

Implementation, UI, validation, testing, deployment and production readiness in a compressed window.

People

Designed for business and operations users rather than expecting them to run a technical script.

Result

Met the deadline with a repeatable, safer and easier workflow.

Follow-ups

  • What scope did you deliberately exclude?
  • How did you test under time pressure?
  • What permissions and audit trail existed?
  • Why was UI worth the extra effort?
  • How did you prevent the emergency solution from becoming fragile?

Master project E: Wally full-stack experience

Supporting storyMobile + streaming

User experience through backend and production

Business

Deliver an AI-assisted user experience that felt responsive and reliable.

Technical

Client/server state, streaming responses, connectivity, recovery, testing, deployment and hypercare.

People

Advocated for the user experience while coordinating backend contracts and release support.

Result

Shipped the application and established the operating path; add measured adoption only if verified.

When to use it

Use Wally for questions about frontend/mobile breadth, streaming, product experience, unfamiliar domains or end-to-end UI-to-backend flow. Do not use it automatically for every ownership question.

Deep-dive self-check

  • Can I explain why the project mattered before naming technologies?
  • Can I separate my decisions from team execution?
  • Can I name one rejected alternative and why it was reasonable?
  • Can I explain one failure mode and recovery path?
  • Can I state one result without inventing a metric?
  • Can I name what I would change now?
  • Can I describe the other person's perspective fairly?
  • Can I give a public-safe version without private identifiers?
