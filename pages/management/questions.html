Evidence labels used in this section

Official means Atlassian publishes the format or prompt family. Candidate-reported means a public first-person or curated report describes it; these reports are self-reported and can vary by level, location and interviewer. Practice variant means an editorial preparation source or a logical follow-up, not proof that Atlassian asks the exact wording.

Official question families

Atlassian publishes four management competencies rather than a fixed script. The questions below paraphrase those competencies into realistic prompts.

Official Drive outcomes across the SDLC
  • Walk me through a project you owned from problem definition to production.
  • What business or customer problem justified the work?
  • What was personally yours, and what did the team own?
  • Which trade-off was hardest? What alternatives did you reject?
  • How did you know the project succeeded?
  • What happened after launch: adoption, incidents, operational load or iteration?
Official Apply and share lessons
  • Tell me about a project that stretched you. What did you learn?
  • Describe a mistake you will not repeat.
  • How did you turn a production issue into a team-level improvement?
  • When have you taught, documented or standardized something for others?
  • How did feedback change your behavior?
Official Manage conflict
  • Tell me about a meaningful disagreement with an engineer, product partner or manager.
  • How did you handle conflicting priorities across teams?
  • Describe a time the team did not support your initial proposal.
  • When did you change your mind because another person had better evidence?
  • How did you preserve trust after a difficult decision?
Official Take initiative, lead and inspire
  • What problem did you address before anyone assigned it to you?
  • How have you led without formal authority?
  • Tell me about a process or architecture change you drove.
  • How have you made another engineer or team more successful?
  • When did you create clarity in an ambiguous situation?

High-probability project deep-dive questions

Project

Why did this work matter?

Start with customer/business pain, not architecture. Quantify scale, risk or cost where verified.

Ownership

What did you personally decide?

Name your decisions, documents, code, coordination and production responsibility. Give team credit separately.

Judgment

What alternatives did you consider?

Compare two credible choices and the constraint that made one preferable.

Impact

How did you measure success?

Use outcome, leading indicators, operational evidence and customer/business validation.

Learning

What would you do differently?

Give a real change, not "nothing." Link it to a concrete guardrail or behavior.

Scope

How did you influence beyond your team?

Explain dependencies, competing incentives, alignment mechanism and durable outcome.

Practice variants by theme

Ownership and delivery
  • What was the hardest point in the project and why?
  • How did you split the work and manage dependencies?
  • What did you cut or defer?
  • How did you launch safely?
  • How did you handle post-launch issues?
Technical judgment
  • Which architecture decision had the most irreversible cost?
  • Where did you accept eventual consistency or operational complexity?
  • What evidence changed your design?
  • How would this design change at ten times scale?
Conflict and influence
  • What exactly did each side want?
  • What did you do before escalating?
  • What part of the other view was valid?
  • Who made the final call?
  • How did you support the decision if it was not yours?
Learning, failure and feedback
  • What signal did you miss?
  • What was your contribution to the failure?
  • How quickly did you communicate it?
  • Which permanent control changed?
  • How did you know the learning spread beyond you?
Mentorship and team growth
  • How did you diagnose the person's real need?
  • Did you teach, coach, pair or delegate?
  • How did you avoid taking over?
  • What changed in the person's independent capability?
Motivation and fit
  • Why Atlassian and why this role now?
  • What type of problem gives you energy?
  • What do you want to learn in the next two years?
  • What team environment helps you do your best work?
  • What would success in your first six months look like?

Follow-up pressure test

  • If you say "we," expect: What did you personally do?
  • If you say "impact," expect: What number or observable change proves it?
  • If you say "conflict," expect: What did the other person believe, and why was that reasonable?
  • If you say "customer," expect: How did you know what customers needed?
  • If you say "leadership," expect: Who became more effective because of you?
  • If you say "failure," expect: What was your responsibility and what changed permanently?
