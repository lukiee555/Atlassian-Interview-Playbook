Press "Draw" and answer without opening your notes.
Walk me through a project you owned end to end. What was personally yours? Tell me about the hardest technical decision you made and the alternative you rejected. Describe a time you influenced several teams without formal authority. Tell me about a disagreement. What did the other person believe, and what happened? Describe a production issue. What was your responsibility, and what changed permanently? Tell me about a process or architecture improvement you initiated. What feedback changed the way you work? Tell me about someone you helped become more effective. Why Atlassian, why full stack, and why now? What do you want to grow into over the next two to three years? Tell me about a time you put a shared team outcome ahead of your preferred work. Describe a customer-first decision where the easiest engineering option was not the right one.

Suggested mock agenda

0-5

Introduction

Project deep dive

Use the pricing fallback/aggregation project as the default. Interrupt repeatedly on personal ownership, trade-off, failure semantics and impact.

25-40

Conflict + leadership

Production disagreement or cross-team triage, then the mentoring or backend-visibility feedback story.

40-50

Failure + learning

International deployment runtime-parity failure; probe accountability, recovery and permanent readiness change.

50-55

Career fit

Why Atlassian, goals, strength and development area.

55-60

Your questions

Ask two thoughtful questions, then close.

Interviewer interruption bank

You keep saying "we." What did you do?

Pause and name your decisions, artifacts, implementation, coordination and production responsibility. Then give the team clear credit for its part.

That sounds like implementation. What was the business impact?

Return to the customer/business invariant, verified scale and operating outcome. If the exact money/time metric is unavailable, state the observable result without inventing a number.

Why did anyone disagree with you?

Present the other position as reasonable under its constraints. A one-sided conflict story signals low empathy and weak judgment.

What did you get wrong?

Own a specific assumption, timing, communication or control gap. Explain the permanent change and later evidence.

How did another person become more successful?

Give evidence of increased independence, capability, confidence, delivery or ownership - not only that you answered questions.

Would you make the same decision today?

Separate the decision quality at the time from what newer evidence would change. Avoid rewriting history.

Scorecard

Ownership

Could a listener distinguish your work from the team's?

Impact

Did you connect delivery to a verified customer/business/operational result?

Judgment

Did you compare real alternatives and costs?

People

Did you show listening, influence, credit and trust?

Learning

Did behavior or a system permanently change?

Control

Was the answer concise enough for follow-ups?

Questions to ask the engineering manager

  • What product and technical outcomes would define success in the first six months?
  • Where does this team currently need stronger end-to-end ownership?
  • How are architecture decisions made when product teams and platform teams have different priorities?
  • What differentiates engineers who grow successfully at this level?
  • Which customer or reliability problem is most important for the team this year?

Final two-minute close

The pattern across my strongest work is that I take ownership beyond implementation: I clarify the outcome, make trade-offs explicit, align dependencies, ship with rollback and observability, and stay with the system in production. I am looking to apply that full-stack and operational experience to a team where collaboration and customer impact are central, while continuing to grow my ability to lead across teams.
