How this section is organized

Atlassian's four official Management pillars

Use these as the scoring rubric

The candidate guide says this structured 60-minute interview digs into four primary areas. Choose projects where you had measurable impact, drove improvements, felt stretched, and worked with teammates, other teams or customers to create a meaningful outcome.

01

Own outcomes toward a team goal

Your evidence: pricing continuity/aggregation end-to-end ownership; Wally architecture-to-hypercare as a full-stack backup.

02

Use expertise to drive improvements

Your evidence: five Spark jobs consolidated into one large-scale pipeline; pricing correctness and migration improvements.

03

Take initiative for positive change

Your evidence: Promo operations UI instead of a script-only handoff; volunteering to stabilize rollout triage.

04

Contribute to team and organization success

Your evidence: mentoring junior/vendor engineers, cross-team pricing integration, and sustained defect triage across a large rollout.

Also assessed

Be ready to explain how you collaborate, what work gives you energy, your ambitions, and why this Full-Stack role is the right next step. Use STAR-L, but keep the answer centered on your ownership, measurable outcome, judgment and learning.

Choose the story from the question

"Most complex project"

Pricing fallback and derived-price aggregation. Use Wally only when the interviewer specifically wants user-facing/mobile breadth.

"A change you initiated"

Spark consolidation. Backup: operations UI instead of a script-only process.

"A difficult technical decision"

Database read versus in-memory cache; explicit retry versus silent degraded pricing; staged modes versus big-bang migration.

"Feedback you received"

Backend contributions were not visible; you changed how you document, present and seek architectural review.

"A failure you owned"

International production deployment failed because runtime parity had not been validated.

"Conflict with senior people"

Production containment was acceptable temporarily, but permanent behavior needed business confirmation.

"Helping someone succeed"

One-sprint coaching for a technically capable engineer who struggled to communicate and demonstrate implementation ownership.

"Team before self"

Volunteered for cross-team issue triage when the rotation was not working.

"Customer-first choice"

Primary: fail/retry rather than silently publish an incomplete derived price. Backup: Promo UI; Wally streaming.

STAR-L plus three lenses

SSituation

Business/customer context and the real tension. Keep this short.

TTask

Your responsibility, authority, constraints and what success meant.

AAction

What you decided, rejected, communicated, implemented and changed.

RResult

Verified customer, business, operational and team outcomes.

LLearning

What changed permanently in how you work.

Three lenses for every deep dive

Business: why this mattered. Technical: the hard decision and trade-off. People: who depended on you, disagreed, or became more effective.

A likely 60-minute flow

0–5 min

Introduction and role fit

Pricing-first career narrative, why full stack, why Atlassian and hands-on technical-lead direction.

5–25 min

Project deep dive

Prepare the pricing fallback/aggregation story as the default master project.

25–45 min

Behavioral scenarios

Feedback, failure, conflict, mentorship, initiative, team-first work and customer judgment.

45–55 min

Motivation and career direction

Senior/staff IC path, broader product ownership and cross-team technical influence.

55–60 min

Your questions

Ask about scope, ownership, decision-making, collaboration and what strong performance looks like.

Questions to ask the manager

Strong performance

What differentiates engineers who perform strongly at this level from those who grow into the next level?

Scope and ownership

How does the team balance ownership of individual services with customer outcomes that cross multiple teams?

Interview confidentiality guard

Use public-safe abstractions

This site is suitable for GitHub Pages, so the personalized scripts avoid repository names, branch names, exact configuration keys, private store identifiers and internal company names. In the interview, describe the architecture and decision precisely while keeping proprietary identifiers generalized.
