The five values translated into behavior

O

Open company, no bullshit

Behavior: surface uncomfortable facts early, challenge respectfully, share reasoning and adapt communication to the audience.

Primary story: incident containment versus permanent business behavior.

Backup: feedback about backend visibility.

H

Build with heart and balance

Behavior: move with urgency while protecting the risks that are expensive or irreversible.

Primary story: pricing fallback and aggregation design.

Backup: staged migration modes.

C

Don't #@!% the customer

Behavior: protect the customer-visible invariant, even when the convenient internal solution appears acceptable.

Primary story: retry instead of silently under-pricing from partial data.

Backups: Promo UI; Wally streaming.

T

Play, as a team

Behavior: optimize the shared outcome, own gaps between teams and help others become effective.

Primary story: cross-team rollout triage.

Backup: mentoring engineers.

B

Be the change you seek

Behavior: identify accepted friction and create a durable better way rather than waiting for instruction.

Primary story: Spark consolidation.

Backups: Promo UI; changing your own visibility habits after feedback.

The controlled 90-second answer

Set the tensionWhat two legitimate goals were in conflict?
Name your responsibilityWhat did you personally own or decide?
Show observable behaviorWhat did you say, ask, change, build or enable?
Give the resultCustomer, business, team or operational evidence.
ReflectWhat behavior do you repeat now?

How to choose when the prompt is unfamiliar

Directness, transparency or challenging someone

Use the production disagreement. Show respect for urgency and decision ownership.

Trade-off, pressure or speed

Use pricing failure semantics, staged migration or the two-day UI depending on whether the prompt is technical, delivery or user-focused.

Customer advocacy

Use the pricing invariant for correctness, the UI for usability, or Wally for responsiveness.

Team success or sacrifice

Use triage when asked about team before self; mentoring when asked how you helped a person.

Initiative or change

Use Spark for system/process change; feedback story for changing yourself.

Common failure modes

  • Defining the value instead of telling a story.
  • Turning the answer into architecture with no human choice.
  • Making the other person obviously wrong.
  • Using “we” so often that your behavior is invisible.
  • Claiming customer focus without identifying the customer-visible consequence.
  • Equating weekend work with passion or team value.
  • Using an unverified result to make the story sound complete.
  • Forcing the same story into all five values.
