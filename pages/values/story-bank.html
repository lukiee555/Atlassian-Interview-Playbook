Story 1 · Pricing fallback and derived-price aggregation

Confirmed facts

Multi-stage event flow; explicit normal/replay/emergency modes; stage-aware routing; current-state database read; no-ratchet aggregation; per-offer failure isolation; retriable message signal; broad automated tests; end-to-end implementation and deployment.

Best tension

Keep pricing flowing without silently using incomplete data or intercepting the wrong stage.

Best result

Simplified legacy handling, improved derived-price accuracy and safer rollback/replay.

Risk

Internal identifiers and exact topology should be generalized for a public interview or GitHub Pages site.

Story 2 · Five Spark jobs to one

Confirmed facts

Five related jobs were consolidated; unified pipeline processed more than 165 million records.

Best tension

Simplify operations without erasing real differences or creating an uncontrolled failure domain.

Best result

One pipeline, simpler ownership and change surface.

Risk

Do not invent compute savings or failure-rate improvements.

Story 3 · Staged migration and tenant integration

Confirmed facts

Control/API-only, parallel-comparison and target/database-only modes; multi-market and tenant-context dependencies.

Best tension

Move quickly while retaining comparison, rollback and cross-service correctness.

Best result

Observable migration path and clearer contracts across teams.

Risk

Be ready to explain cleanup of temporary modes.

Story 4 · International production deployment failure

Confirmed facts

Java upgrade; testing passed in lower environments and one market; international production pipeline lacked a required runtime option.

Your ownership

You owned production delivery and had not validated runtime parity.

Learning

Readiness includes runtime, deployment pipeline and market-specific parity—not only functional tests.

Risk

Do not blame the colleague who worked on the original upgrade.

Story 5 · Production disagreement

Confirmed facts

Engineering proposed a fast behavior during an incident; you opposed making it a permanent business decision; temporary containment was used and business owners confirmed final behavior.

Best tension

Urgent containment versus correct decision ownership.

Behavior

Challenge respectfully, acknowledge urgency, separate temporary and permanent decisions.

Risk

Explain why senior engineers' view was reasonable.

Story 6 · Cross-team rollout triage

Confirmed facts

Large release, many teams, unstable rotation, constant defects, you volunteered for sustained initial triage and coordination.

Best tension

Your own implementation work versus shared release flow.

Result

Cleaner ownership and less repeated reassignment.

Risk

Use “many teams” unless the participant count is verified.

Story 7 · Mentoring

Confirmed facts

Mentored five to six teammates across junior, vendor and frontend roles; recently coached one technically capable person for a sprint on communication and implementation ownership.

Best tension

Knowledge existed, but confidence and communication prevented visible ownership.

Action

Psychological safety, reasoning before solution, early blockers, targeted review and space to present.

Missing

One observed result after the sprint.

Story 8 · Backend-visibility feedback

Confirmed facts

Senior leadership primarily saw frontend contribution; you began design documents, solution presentations and regular reviews with principal/staff/distinguished engineers.

Best tension

Strong work was not sufficiently reviewable or visible.

Result

Broader trust and visibility as a full-stack engineer.

Learning

Communication and documentation are part of technical leadership.

Story 9 · Promo operations UI

Confirmed facts

Business needed a missing solution before Monday; you completed implementation, testing and deployment within two days and added a UI rather than only handing over a script.

Best tension

Fastest engineering closure versus safest workflow for the user.

Result

Deadline met with a repeatable, non-technical user experience.

Risk

Do not use weekend effort as the main impact statement.

Story 10 · Wally streaming

Confirmed facts

Simple approach was to wait for the complete model output; you advocated for streaming and owned end-to-end delivery.

Best tension

Implementation simplicity versus perceived responsiveness.

Result

More responsive user interaction; use qualitative language unless measured.

Risk

Do not let Wally displace stronger pricing examples for backend ownership.

Practise every story at three lengths

30 seconds

Problem, decision, result.

90 seconds

Tension, role, three actions, result, learning.

3 minutes

Add other perspective, rejected alternative, technical or people detail and what you would change.

Follow-up mode

Answer the exact probe in 20–40 seconds rather than retelling the story.

Authenticity audit

  • Can I explain why the other choice was reasonable?
  • Can I name what I personally said or decided?
  • Can I state a result without exaggeration?
  • Can I explain one thing I got wrong?
  • Can I give credit to others?
  • Can I answer without exposing private identifiers?
  • Can I use a different story when the question changes?
