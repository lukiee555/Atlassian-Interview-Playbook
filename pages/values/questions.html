Evidence labels used in this section

Official means Atlassian publishes the format or prompt family. Candidate-reported means a public first-person or curated report describes it; these reports are self-reported and can vary by level, location and interviewer. Practice variant means an editorial preparation source or a logical follow-up, not proof that Atlassian asks the exact wording.

Official prompt families

The wording below is a concise paraphrase of Atlassian's published guide. The interviewer may use different wording or combine values.

Official Be the change you seek
  • When have you advocated for change?
  • What did you learn while pushing for it?
  • What outcome followed?

Follow-ups to expect: Why had nobody changed it earlier? Who resisted? What did you do beyond proposing an idea? Did the change persist? What would you change now?

Official Play, as a team
  • How do you work with other people?
  • How have you helped a person or team succeed when performance was below expectations?
  • When did you put the team's goals above personal ambition?

Follow-ups to expect: What did the person need from you? What did you sacrifice? How did you avoid taking over? What evidence shows the team became stronger?

Official Don't #@!% the customer
  • How is your mentality user-centered?
  • When have you put the customer first?
  • What trade-off did you make to do that?

Follow-ups to expect: How did you know what customers needed? Which customers? What did the business want instead? Did you delay delivery, reduce scope or accept cost?

Official Open company, no bullshit
  • How do you approach difficult conversations?
  • How have you resolved conflict?
  • When did you adapt your communication style to improve a negative situation?

Follow-ups to expect: What exactly did you say? Why was it hard? What was your contribution to the issue? What did the other person hear? What would they say about you afterward?

Official Build with heart and balance
  • How do you approach prioritization and decision-making?
  • When did you or the team miss the mark?
  • How did you react and fix it?

Follow-ups to expect: Which option did you deliberately not choose? Where did urgency matter? Where did you slow down? What guardrail prevented over-engineering or burnout?

Source: official Fullstack Engineering Interview Guide.

Practice question bank by value

Open company

Directness with care

  • Tell me about a risk you had to surface when the team wanted to move quickly.
  • Describe a difficult conversation you initially avoided.
  • When did you admit that your own decision was wrong?
  • How have you communicated bad news to stakeholders?
  • Tell me about feedback that was hard to hear.
  • When did you disagree and still commit?
Heart + balance

Passion with judgment

  • Tell me about balancing speed with quality.
  • When did you deliberately reduce scope?
  • Describe a decision where temporary complexity made the rollout safer.
  • When did the team miss the mark, and how did you recover?
  • How do you avoid over-engineering?
  • When did you protect sustainable execution under pressure?
Customer

Trust before convenience

  • Tell me about a customer issue you personally drove to resolution.
  • When did you challenge a plan because of customer risk?
  • Describe a trade-off that protected correctness or reliability.
  • When did customer evidence change your implementation?
  • How do you represent customers when you do not meet them directly?
  • Tell me about a time you made recovery or rollback easier for customers.
Team

Shared outcome first

  • Tell me about helping someone become independently effective.
  • When did you put a team objective ahead of personal visibility?
  • Describe collaboration across teams with different incentives.
  • When did you change your approach after hearing a teammate?
  • How have you handled an underperforming teammate?
  • Tell me about sharing credit after a difficult delivery.
Change

Improve without waiting

  • What recurring problem did you fix without being assigned?
  • Tell me about changing a process that others had accepted.
  • When did you build evidence before advocating for change?
  • Describe a change that outlasted the original project.
  • When was your change initially rejected?
  • How did you know the improvement was worth its adoption cost?

Cross-value follow-ups

What made this genuinely difficult?

Name the competing valid goals: speed versus safety, local delivery versus shared contract, directness versus relationship, individual progress versus team recovery, or short-term cost versus customer trust.

What did you personally do?

Use verbs attached to artifacts and conversations: I mapped, proposed, asked, wrote, compared, facilitated, implemented, instrumented, escalated or followed through.

What was the other perspective?

Present it fairly. The value signal is weak when the alternative is obviously careless or irrational.

What would the other person say?

Show awareness of impact, not just intent. Explain how you checked understanding and preserved trust.

What did you learn?

Name a specific behavior or mechanism you now repeat: scenario-based decision records, staged rollout, earlier risk communication, batch authorization, post-incident guardrails or coaching through questions.

Would you act the same way today?

Separate what was reasonable with the information available from what later evidence taught you.

Question selection rule

Choose for tension, not prestige

A smaller story with a clear behavioral choice is stronger than a large project where the value was never tested. The interviewer needs to see what you do when two reasonable goals collide.
